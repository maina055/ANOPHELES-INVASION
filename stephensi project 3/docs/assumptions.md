# Assumptions and interpretation

## A1 Populations and mixing

Two populations initially contain 100,000 residents each. Exposure is homogeneous within each setting apart from age-dependent biting. Movement, household heterogeneity and mosquito dispersal are excluded. Results are hypothetical scenarios, not county estimates.

## A2 Demography

Birth intensity is 0.027 per person-year and natural mortality 1/68 per year. Births and childhood ageing occur at monthly boundaries; maternal protection and congenital infection are absent. The 5–14-year group ages continuously at 1/(10 × 365.25) per day. Age shares and representative older ages are assumed. Natural growth changes the population while vector capacities remain fixed.

## A3 Infection and immunity

The untreated blood-stage state includes symptomatic and asymptomatic infections. All treated survivors clear into the recovered state. Reference recovery gives complete temporary infection blocking for a mean 180 days; alternatives allow 50% or full susceptible-level reinfection. There is no acquired anti-disease immunity history, concurrent superinfection, recrudescence, prophylaxis compartment or explicit gametocyte tail. The five-day treated state is a simplified transferred model duration, not a clinical prescribing recommendation.

## A4 Observation and calibration

Microscopy positivity is approximated by all living blood-stage infections, including treated infections. Species fractions are transferred across ages. Reference microscopy sensitivity and specificity are one; lower sensitivity is explored with specificity fixed at one. Two density parameters fit two targets but are not independently identified from biting or infection duration. Survey-based starting states are not pre-rollout counterfactuals or endemic equilibria.

## A5 Vector ecology

Native transmission is represented by one effective vector system. The invader is restricted to urban residents and introduced with 4,500 susceptible adults and 3,000 immature females. Capacity-setting abundance, mortality and biting are assumptions, not observed Kenyan invasion estimates. Seasonality, climate variability, competition and stochastic extinction are omitted.

## A6 Delivery

Doses occur at 6, 7, 9 and 24 months with sequential receipt. Children missing a dose do not receive later doses; there is no catch-up, delayed initiation, stock-out or repeat fourth dose. Coverage is historical and common to settings and infection states. Programme totals reflect cohort entry and incomplete follow-up.

## A7 Vaccine protection

Doses 3–4 reduce acquisition through a fitted two-component mean profile transferred from Kilifi antibody inputs. They neither clear existing infections nor independently reduce clinical severity. Doses 1–2 have zero reference protection; exploratory alternatives do not constitute measured early-dose efficacy. Mixture components approximate population mean waning, not continuously declining individual protection.

## A8 Clinical outcomes

Clinical and severe probabilities are source immunity-category values mapped to two age groups. Reference fatality is 7% at all ages, transferred from paediatric inpatient evidence. Clinical outcomes and deaths occur at blood-stage onset. Severe disease is an outcome mapping rather than a separate dynamic state. Full episode disability is assigned even to fatal severe episodes. Age-specific multipliers test sensitivity; they do not replace clinical calibration.

## A9 Care and admission

KMIS under-five fever care-seeking proportions proxy effective uncomplicated malaria care and are transferred across ages. All severe episodes are admitted in the reference scenario. Surviving episodes assigned care enter a two-day waiting state and a five-day treated state. Waiting infections have untreated infectiousness; treated infections have relative infectiousness 0.3221. Costs are assigned at care allocation, even if natural death precedes completion. In the reduced-admission alternative, admissions and inpatient costs fall, untreated severe mortality doubles and transmission is rerun.

## A10 Control

The hypothetical bundle immediately and continuously reduces invader biting and carrying capacity while increasing adult mortality. Operational effects may overlap. No implementation delay, waning, resistance or additional effect on native vectors is modelled. The cost–effect combinations are stress tests, not measured performance or a procurement recommendation.

## A11 Economics

Historical provider costs and dose costs are applied proportionally, with paediatric costs transferred across ages. The inpatient proxy represents selected severe phenotypes, not all admissions. Fixed programme costs, capacity constraints, household costs, adverse events and neurological sequelae are omitted. Remaining lifetime uses coarse group values and a constant future-loss stream. This is an economic evaluation, not a financial budget.

## A12 Comparisons and uncertainty

Matched scenarios differ only in specified inputs. Outcomes per completed course include indirect effects and partial vaccination. Survey envelopes, structural alternatives and Latin-hypercube ranges are scenarios, not empirical confidence or prediction intervals. No probability of cost-effectiveness, optimal national programme or equity ranking is estimated.
